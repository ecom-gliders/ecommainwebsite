## What experienced Amazon sellers check before entering a niche

Treat niche entry as an investment decision, not a guess. Soft claim: disciplined checks reduce inventory risk; they do not guarantee seven-figure outcomes. This guide lists pre-entry checks. For signal reading see [research demand and competition](/smart-way-to-research-demand-and-competition-on-amazon/); for validation order see [step-by-step product validation](/step-by-step-method-to-validate-products-on-amazon/); for finding candidates see [how to find profitable products on Amazon](/how-to-find-profitable-products-on-amazon/).

## Demand beyond search volume

Look for buyer intent, consistency, and stability—not only large keyword numbers.

*   Search interest that holds across roughly 12 months when history exists
*   Stable or improving Best Seller Rank patterns
*   Several listings with ongoing sales, not one monopoly
*   Limited dependence on constant discounts or extreme PPC to move units

Consumables, refills, and accessories can support repeat demand—**Assumed** by category, not universal.

## Profitability after Amazon costs

Model contribution after referral and FBA fees, inbound and domestic shipping, storage (including long-term risk), advertising needed for visibility, and returns. Soft heuristic (**Requires confirmation**): many operators walk away when contribution after ads looks thin under competitive spend—not because a fixed 25–35% net rule is verified for every niche. High revenue with thin margins is fragile in a price war.

## Review mining for improvement room

Pattern-hunt negative and neutral reviews for quality, sizing, durability, packaging, missing features, and failed use cases. Soft claim: enter with a better deliverable version when gaps are real; if you cannot improve the offer, reconsider the niche.

## Keyword depth and traffic mix

Prefer niches where sales come from many mid- and long-tail terms—not one expensive head keyword. Check keyword diversity, PPC density and CPC pressure, organic difficulty on mid-volume terms, and whether off-Amazon traffic is plausible. Soft claim: distributed traffic can reduce single-keyword ad dependency over time.

## Brandability and expansion

Ask whether related SKUs, bundles, or complementary products are realistic, whether the audience is specific enough to message clearly, and whether the category supports a small line—not only one orphan ASIN. Soft claim: expansion options help; they do not create automatic brand scale.

## Bottom line

Demand, economics, review gaps, keyword structure, and expansion path should all clear before a purchase order. Soft outcome: fewer blind launches—not guaranteed long-term growth.

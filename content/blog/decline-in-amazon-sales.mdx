## Soft ways “decline” gets misread

*   **YoY after a surge year** — post-pandemic normalization can look like decline versus an inflated base—**Assumed**  
*   **Units vs revenue** — higher ASPs can hold revenue while unit volume softens  
*   **Category ≠ marketplace** — discretionary categories may soften while consumables stay steadier—**Assumed**  
*   **Your ASIN ≠ Amazon** — stockouts, ad cuts, or more competitors can drop *your* sales while category demand is flat  

## Soft seller diagnostic checklist

1. **Inventory** — stockouts, stranded offers, restock limits  
2. **Conversion** — reviews, price vs competitors, listing content, Buy Box  
3. **Traffic** — organic rank moves, brand vs non-brand share  
4. **Ads** — budget throttling, ACoS/TACoS spikes, wasted search terms  
5. **Fees and price** — fee/storage/ad cost inflation pushing price up and CVR down  
6. **Seasonality and events** — compare to same weeks last year, not only last month  
7. **Competition** — new entrants, copycats, coupon intensity  

Soft claim: most recoverable drops show up in one of these buckets—**Assumed**.

## Soft macro themes (context only)

Inflation and tighter household budgets can pressure discretionary spend. Soft claim: social/discovery commerce can divert attention before purchase; Amazon may still close the transaction—**Assumed**. Soft claim: rising seller costs and ad competition can reduce unit velocity without proving platform failure.

## Soft what not to do

Do not treat anonymous forums or unverified percentage graphics as marketplace truth. Soft claim: any “40% / 70% / 90%” style figures without sources are **not verified**. Soft practice: use Seller Central business reports and Amazon Ads data for *your* account first.

## Bottom line

Ask whether the decline is **your account**, **your category**, or a **headline about Amazon**. Soft claim: structured diagnosis beats panic; platform-wide collapse claims need public evidence—**Requires confirmation**.

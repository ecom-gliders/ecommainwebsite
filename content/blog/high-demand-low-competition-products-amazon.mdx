## How to find high-demand, low-competition products on Amazon

“High demand, low competition” is a research filter—not a guaranteed niche. Soft claim: look for buyer intent with real sales velocity plus weak listing quality, then confirm contribution after ads. Related: [why sellers fail product research](/why-amazon-sellers-fail-product-research/), [avoid saturated niche price wars](/avoid-saturated-niche-price-wars-amazon/), and [choose winning products without guesswork](/choose-winning-products-without-guesswork/).

![Finding high-demand, low-competition Amazon products.](/images/blog/WhatsApp-Image-2026-01-02-at-8.46.55-AM-56b33ef2.jpeg)

## Confirm demand with buyer intent

High search volume alone is not demand. Prefer signals that buyers are purchasing:

*   Best Seller Rank stability over time (not one spike)
*   Sales velocity even when listing creative is weak
*   Seasonality checked across roughly 12–24 months of history when available

Soft observation (**Assumed**): several mediocre listings selling steadily can hint that demand exists and presentation is unfinished—still validate economics before you source.

## Measure competition beyond review count

Low competition means weak rivals, not empty search results. Evaluate:

*   Image, copy, and A+ gaps
*   Whether a few strong brands already own the SERP
*   Review velocity, not only total reviews
*   Price stability (constant undercutting can signal a price war)

Soft heuristic only (**Requires confirmation**): fragmented brands and unfinished creatives can mean room to enter; fixed review-count cutoffs are not rules.

## Use keywords for gaps

Favor long-tail purchase intent (for example “foldable laptop stand for travel”) over head terms alone. Look for demand where titles rarely cover the phrase and paid density looks manageable. Soft claim: organic rank without heavy ads can reflect weak competition—or low demand—so cross-check velocity.

## Mine reviews for product gaps

Reviews are free research. Pattern-hunt:

*   Repeated complaints across multiple ASINs
*   Features buyers ask for but do not get
*   Quality issues buyers tolerate for lack of alternatives

A better-engineered answer can differentiate without racing on price alone—soft claim, not “instant” wins.

## Validate profitability last

Demand plus weak competition still fails without margin. Check landed cost, fees, ads, returns, shipping profile, MOQ, and whether variations or bundles can expand the line. Soft heuristic (**Requires confirmation**): model contribution after ads rather than a fixed 30% net target.

## Bottom line

High-demand, low-competition finds are combinations of intent, velocity, weak listing quality, keyword gaps, and workable economics—not a product list you can copy.

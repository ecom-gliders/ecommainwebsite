## What is Amazon PPC for beginners?

Amazon PPC is pay-per-click advertising where you pay when a shopper clicks your ad. For beginners in 2026, start with Sponsored Products, run auto campaigns for discovery, move winners into manual campaigns, and review search terms weekly. Ads buy visibility while listings convert; weak listings waste budget.

If you launched without ads, or ads feel confusing, this guide covers campaign types, match types, a simple first setup, bidding options, ACOS, and common mistakes. ## What is Amazon PPC?

Amazon PPC places your products in sponsored placements in search results or on product pages. You pay for clicks, not impressions alone.

Main ad types:

*   **Sponsored Products** - Individual ASIN ads in search and on product pages
*   **Sponsored Brands** - Brand logo, headline, and multiple products (and related creative formats when eligible)
*   **Sponsored Display** - Audience and product targeting on and off Amazon

Beginners should start with Sponsored Products. It is the clearest path from spend to sales data. Comparison: [Sponsored Brands vs Sponsored Products](/amazon-sponsored-brands-vs-products/).

## Why PPC matters in 2026

Organic page-one ranking for new listings is often slow. PPC can create immediate visibility and sales velocity that supports relevance signals while you improve SEO.

Optimize the listing before spending. Ads amplify what the page already is. Listing help: [Amazon listing optimization](/amazon-listing-optimization/).

## Auto vs manual campaigns

### Auto campaign

Amazon chooses placements based on listing content. You set budget and a default bid.

**Pros:** Faster start, useful for discovering real shopper terms, helpful for new ASINs.

**Cons:** Less control, more irrelevant traffic risk, needs regular negatives.

### Manual campaign

You choose keywords or product targets and control bids more tightly.

**Pros:** Better control and scaling once you know converters.

**Cons:** Needs research and more management time.

**Practical sequence:** Run auto for discovery, then move strong search terms into manual phrase or exact campaigns.

## Keyword match types

*   **Broad** - Widest reach; more irrelevant queries possible
*   **Phrase** - Search includes your phrase in order; tighter than broad
*   **Exact** - Closest match; usually most efficient once proven

A beginner approach: gather data with auto and controlled phrase/broad, then shift budget to exact on clear converters. Negatives: [Amazon negative keywords 2026](/amazon-negative-keywords-2026/).

## Setting up your first campaign

1.  **Optimize the listing first** - Title, bullets, images, and A+ Content where available.
2.  **Set a realistic daily budget** - Many beginners start small per campaign (for example about $10 to $20/day) and adjust after data appears. Category CPCs vary.
3.  **Launch an auto campaign** - Use close match, loose match, substitutes, and complements with a conservative default bid for your category. Let it run long enough to collect search terms.
4.  **Analyze the search term report** - Move converting terms to manual; negate irrelevant or high-spend zero-sale terms after enough clicks.
5.  **Build a manual campaign** - Exact or phrase on top converters with bids guided by auto performance.

## Bid strategy basics

*   **Dynamic bids - down only** - Amazon can lower bids when a click looks less likely to convert. Often the safest beginner default.
*   **Dynamic bids - up and down** - Can raise bids for likely conversions. Use only with strong conversion data.
*   **Fixed bids** - Bid stays as set. Less protection for beginners.

Start with down only on discovery campaigns until you have enough history to test more aggressive options.

## Understanding ACOS

**ACOS = Ad spend / Ad sales x 100**

Example: $20 spend and $100 sales = 20% ACOS.

Target ACOS depends on margin after fees and COGS. Beginners often use break-even ACOS as a ceiling, then tighten as structure improves. More: [lower Amazon ACOS without losing sales](/lower-amazon-acos-without-losing-sales/).

## Common beginner mistakes

*   Ads on an unoptimized listing
*   Set-and-forget without weekly search-term review
*   Broad manual targeting too early
*   Ignoring negative keywords
*   Pausing campaigns before enough data (often at least two weeks on auto, longer when volume is low)

## When to scale

After 30 to 60 days of usable data, consider scaling when ACOS is near target, exact converters are stable, and organic movement is not collapsing. Raise budgets in modest steps rather than doubling overnight.

## Bottom line

Amazon PPC is manageable when you start simple: one auto campaign, one manual campaign for winners, a clear budget, and a weekly review habit. Consistency with search-term data usually beats large budgets without structure.

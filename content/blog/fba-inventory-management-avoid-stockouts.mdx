## Soft Amazon constraints to watch

*   **IPI / inventory health** — poor sell-through or aged stock can tighten storage and raise stockout risk—**Assumed**  
*   **Restock limits** — align inbound to velocity and age; ignored limits delay replenishment  
*   **FC redistribution latency** — units can be “in network” but not yet sellable; plan for check-in and transfer delay  

## Soft stockout impact (checklist)

Organic rank decay while OOS; Sponsored ads may pause; Buy Box eligibility drops; velocity reset after restock often needs extra paid support—**Assumed**. Soft practice: treat stockouts as costly recovery events, not only missed daily sales.

## Practical pillars (soft)

1. **90-day rolling forecast** — update weekly; separate promo/ad-driven demand from baseline  
2. **True position** — sellable FBA + near-term inbound − reserved/unsellable + in-transit awareness  
3. **Reorder point** — average daily sales × lead time + safety stock; retune when lead time changes  
4. **Tiered safety stock** — stable / growth / peak / promo layers instead of one flat buffer  
5. **Rolling POs** — continuous supplier cadence beats sporadic emergency orders  
6. **Dual source high-velocity SKUs** — reduce single-supplier failure modes  
7. **Amazon restock suggestions as secondary** — validate, do not sole-source decisions  
8. **Off-Amazon buffer** — small 3PL/supplier reserve for emergencies when economics allow  
9. **Ops controls** — capacity monitoring, reserved-inventory checks, low-stock alerts, supplier scoring  

## Soft formula reminder

Reorder point ≈ (average daily sales × lead time) + safety stock. Soft claim: under-sized safety stock during volatility is a common stockout driver—**Assumed**.

## Bottom line

Use a practical FBA checklist: forecast, position, reorder, buffers, and supplier redundancy. Soft outcome: fewer stockouts and cleaner IPI—not guaranteed zero OOS.

## What is Amazon Account Health Rating?

Amazon Account Health Rating (AHR) is a Seller Central score that reflects Amazon’s assessment of how reliably you meet marketplace and customer-experience standards. Sellers often watch the number without connecting it to open issues, severity, and recency. AHR is a risk indicator—not a trophy for activity, appeals filed, or effort alone.

Exact weighting and thresholds can change. Confirm current definitions and status colors in Seller Central’s Account Health dashboard. This guide is operational framing, not Amazon legal advice.

## What AHR typically reflects

AHR generally sits on a 0–1000 scale and updates as issues open, resolve, and age. A strong historical record does not erase the impact of a serious open violation today. Amazon is assessing risk to customers and to marketplace integrity—not intentions.

## Issue types that usually matter most

Amazon groups account-health issues into categories in Seller Central. For day-to-day management, triage by customer impact:

### Customer-trust and authenticity issues

These often carry the heaviest consequences and slower recovery when they involve authenticity, intellectual property, safety, or similar customer-harm themes. Examples sellers commonly monitor:

*   Inauthentic item complaints
*   Used sold as new
*   Intellectual property complaints
*   Safety-related product issues

One severe issue in this family can outweigh many minor operational misses. After resolution, recovery may still take time if Amazon continues to weigh recent history.

### Fulfillment and order-defect patterns

Often includes metrics such as:

*   Late Shipment Rate
*   Order Defect Rate
*   Cancellation Rate

Occasional operational slips differ from sustained patterns. A bad week can be recoverable; a bad month may signal a process problem. FBA does not remove all account-health exposure when customer outcomes are affected.

### Policy and documentation hygiene

Examples:

*   Missing or incomplete compliance documents
*   Expired certifications where required
*   Minor listing-policy flags

These can block selling or erode confidence without always matching the shock of a severe authenticity or IP event. Treat them on a schedule with clear owners—do not ignore them because the score still looks “mostly fine.”

## What does not automatically raise AHR

Useful corrections:

*   Closing a case alone is not a score boost
*   Filing an appeal is not the same as fixing the root cause
*   Past “good performance” is not a buffer against a severe new violation
*   Dashboard green status on unrelated metrics does not cancel an open high-severity issue

Amazon responds to outcomes and sustained clean operation more than to volume of messages.

## Severity and recency

Two similar issues can land differently if one is recent and one is months old. Recent problems are stronger predictive signals. Older issues may weigh less if they do not repeat. Spacing and non-recurrence usually matter more than a flurry of rushed appeals.

## Why some accounts recover faster

Recovery speed often tracks:

*   Whether the same issue type repeats
*   Whether the stated root cause matches evidence
*   Whether operations stay stable after the fix

Repeated variations of the same failure slow trust rebuild. One credible system fix beats five shallow appeals.

## How to manage AHR in practice

1. Triage: customer-trust and authenticity issues first
2. Fix systems (sourcing, listing claims, fulfillment SOPs), not only the ticket text
3. Contain related catalog risk after one ASIN is flagged
4. Submit factual, evidence-backed appeals when required
5. Accept that score recovery can lag behind operational recovery

## A “perfect” score is not the goal

AHR is a risk indicator. Many healthy accounts operate below 1000 with low-severity, well-managed issues and no active suspension risk. Chasing a perfect number while neglecting inventory, listings, or margin is the wrong tradeoff. Predictable compliance and customer outcomes matter more than vanity perfection.

## Operating checklist

*   Review Account Health weekly with named owners
*   Keep invoices and authorization docs audit-ready
*   Pre-check new ASINs for restricted-product and claim risk
*   Track ODR, late shipment, and cancellation trends before they compound
*   Document root causes when notices appear

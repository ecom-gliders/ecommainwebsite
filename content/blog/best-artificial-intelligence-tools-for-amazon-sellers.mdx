## Soft what makes an AI feature useful

*   **Predictive views** — demand, inventory cover, or ad efficiency with explicit assumptions  
*   **Bounded automation** — keyword harvest, bid rules, reprice with human approval gates  
*   **Fresh marketplace inputs** — price, rank, inventory, listing health where APIs allow  
*   **Actionable recommendations** — not dashboards alone  
*   **Clean Seller Central / Ads integration** — without brittle scrapers when possible  

## Soft capability map (not a ranked “best” list)

| Job | Soft AI-assisted uses | Soft caution |
|-----|----------------------|--------------|
| Product research | Trend and gap signals | Estimates are not verified sales |
| Listing assist | Draft titles/bullets/A+ variants | Policy + brand voice review required |
| PPC | Bid rules, search-term clustering | Guardrails and TACoS still human-owned |
| Inventory | Demand alerts, reorder suggestions | Lead time and cash cycle still manual |
| Pricing | Competitive reprice rules | MAP/brand policy risk |

Soft claim: third-party suite names and feature sets change frequently—verify current offerings before purchase—**Requires confirmation**.

## Soft how to choose

Match tools to **business model** (private label vs wholesale vs multi-SKU ops), **budget**, and **control preference** (full auto vs suggest-and-approve). Soft practice: prefer a small stack over overlapping products that conflict on data.

## Soft mistakes

100% hands-off automation; publishing unedited AI copy; feeding bad fee/COGS data; stacking too many tools. Soft claim: AI should inform strategy, not replace unit-economics judgment—**Assumed**.

## Soft tradeoffs

Pros: faster research cycles, fewer manual PPC chores, clearer alerts. Cons: cost, learning curve, over-automation risk, and vendor lock-in.

## Bottom line

Select AI by job-to-be-done and governance, not hype rankings. Soft claim: that improves decision speed; it does not guarantee growth or replace Amazon policy compliance.

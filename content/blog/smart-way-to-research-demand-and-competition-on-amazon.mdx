## How to research demand and competition on Amazon

Demand research is more than high search volume. Soft claim: combine buyer intent, consistency across the year, listing quality, and pricing behavior before you commit capital. This post focuses on reading demand and competition signals. For a full validation sequence, see [step-by-step product validation](/step-by-step-method-to-validate-products-on-amazon/); for niche price wars, see [avoid saturated niches](/avoid-saturated-niche-price-wars-amazon/).

## Demand beyond search volume

Check how often a product sells through the year and whether demand is stable or seasonal. Soft claim (**Assumed**): moderate, consistent demand can beat spike-and-dry patterns for planning inventory.

Useful indicators:

*   Monthly search volume with commercial-intent keywords
*   Best Seller Rank trends over roughly 90–180 days
*   Review growth rate, not only totals
*   Sales velocity compared across similar listings

## Competition as listing strength

Large brand names alone are not a veto. Soft claim: weak creative from a known brand can leave room; strong private-label listings can block a thin niche. Measure content quality, review substance and recency, pricing control, and who owns organic page one. Eligible sellers can use [Product Opportunity Explorer](https://sell.amazon.com/tools/product-opportunity-explorer) for category context.

Soft note: unfinished top listings may signal a gap—not a license to “exploit” buyers.

## BSR and sales distribution

Look across roughly 10–15 top listings, not one hero ASIN. Even sales across several products can hint at broader demand; a single monopoly can mean a harder entry. Soft claim: distribution is directional, not a low-risk guarantee. Track BSR volatility for market instability.

## Pricing power and margins

Strong demand with weak listing quality still fails under a price race. Prefer stable average selling prices, limited sudden drops, and room for bundles or branding. Soft claim: pricing control helps fund PPC; it does not guarantee contribution after ads.

## External and behavioral checks

Read negative reviews for unmet needs, cross-check longer-term interest with search trends, and note repeated improvement requests. Soft claim: multi-channel chatter can support a thesis; it is noisy.

## Bottom line

Stack intent, BSR distribution, listing strength, price behavior, and external clues. Soft outcome: clearer go/no-go decisions—not a growth engine slogan.

## How do you lower Amazon ACOS without losing sales?

Lower Amazon ACOS without losing sales by cutting wasted search terms, adjusting bids by keyword performance, separating match types for cleaner data, and raising listing conversion so the same traffic buys more often. Blanket bid cuts often shrink impressions and sales. Targeted efficiency usually protects volume better.

If Sponsored Products ACOS is compressing margin, but every broad spend cut also cuts sales, you need a measured plan, not only lower bids. ## What is ACOS and why it matters

ACOS (Advertising Cost of Sale) is:

**ACOS = (Ad spend / Ad sales) x 100**

Example: $200 ad spend and $1,000 ad sales equals 20% ACOS.

High ACOS is not always failure. Launches may accept a higher temporary ACOS for visibility. Mature ASINs usually need ACOS at or below break-even after COGS and Amazon fees.

Break-even ACOS (simplified): (Selling price - COGS - Amazon fees) / Selling price, expressed as a percentage. That is the ceiling before ads lose money on attributed sales. Confirm with your full fee and return model.

## Start with a campaign profitability audit

Pull a search term report for the last 30 to 60 days and flag:

*   High spend with zero or very low conversions
*   Clicks without sales on ASINs or queries
*   Campaigns far above break-even ACOS
*   Ad groups mixing high-intent and low-intent themes

You cannot fix what you have not measured. Related: [Amazon PPC not converting](/amazon-ppc-no-conversions-2026/).

## Use negative keywords with discipline

Negatives are one of the fastest ways to lower ACOS without pausing profitable traffic.

*   Review search terms weekly; flag queries with enough clicks and no sales
*   Negate clearly irrelevant patterns (for example free, used, wrong category)
*   Apply campaign-level negatives when the term should never run
*   Keep a living negative list as discovery campaigns run

Do not negate once and forget. New irrelevant queries appear as Amazon tests placements. Guide: [Amazon negative keywords 2026](/amazon-negative-keywords-2026/).

## Adjust bids surgically

Prefer keyword-level changes over account-wide cuts.

*   Reduce bids gradually on keywords above target ACOS
*   Raise bids carefully on keywords that convert well below target
*   Use placement modifiers when Top of Search conversion is weak relative to cost
*   Cap underperforming portfolios or campaigns when needed

Dynamic bids (down only) can help control ACOS when you want Amazon to lower bids on less likely conversions. Change one variable at a time and wait about 7 to 14 days before judging results when volume allows.

## Restructure for cleaner data

Mixing broad, phrase, and exact in one noisy ad group hides winners. A readable structure often includes:

1.  Auto for discovery
2.  Broad for controlled expansion
3.  Phrase for mid-intent coverage
4.  Exact for proven converters with tighter bids

Harvest profitable search terms into exact, then negate them in discovery campaigns so you do not pay twice. Structure detail: [Amazon PPC campaign structure](/amazon-ppc-campaign-structure/).

## Optimize the listing before scaling bids

Low conversion is an ACOS problem as much as a bid problem. If few clickers buy, ACOS stays high even with lower CPCs.

Before spending more, check:

*   Main image clarity and relevance to the query
*   Title with primary intent near the front
*   Bullets that answer top objections
*   A+ Content where eligible
*   Review quality competitive for the category

Better conversion reduces effective ACOS on traffic you already buy. Listing support: [Amazon listing optimization](/amazon-listing-optimization/).

## Common ACOS mistakes

*   Cutting all bids at once and losing velocity
*   Ignoring match-type separation
*   Never reviewing search term reports
*   Optimizing ACOS without watching TACoS and organic impact
*   Setting campaigns and forgetting weekly hygiene

## Bottom line

Lowering Amazon ACOS without losing sales is about spending with clearer return: negatives, keyword-level bids, cleaner structure, and listing conversion. Sellers with steady data habits usually outperform sellers who only slash budgets.

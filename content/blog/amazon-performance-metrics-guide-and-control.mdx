## What are Amazon performance metrics and how do you control them?

Amazon performance metrics are Seller Central indicators of how reliably your operations meet customer-experience and marketplace standards. They help Amazon assess account risk. Revenue can look healthy while metrics still signal instability. Controlling them means assigning owners, watching trends before thresholds breach, and fixing root causes—not only reacting to notifications.

Confirm current target thresholds and definitions in Seller Central. Amazon can update expectations; treat any percentages below as commonly cited operating targets, not a substitute for your live Account Health view.

Related: [Amazon account health](/amazon-account-health/), [Amazon Account Health Rating explained](/amazon-account-health-rating-explained/), and [Amazon account management](/amazon-account-management/).

## Why metrics matter as much as revenue

Amazon’s priority is customer experience. [Seller performance metrics](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G200205250) measure how closely operations align with that goal. A profitable account can still face escalating enforcement if defects, late shipments, or policy issues trend the wrong way. Professional account management treats metrics as first-class operations work.

## Order Defect Rate (ODR)

ODR typically combines negative feedback, A-to-Z Guarantee claims, and credit card chargebacks related to orders. Sellers commonly keep ODR under Amazon’s published target (often cited near 1%) with room to spare.

Control habits:

*   Root-cause every negative feedback theme—not only removal requests
*   Message customers early when delays or fulfillment issues appear
*   Resolve clear seller-fault issues promptly to reduce escalations

Goal: fewer defects, not only faster damage control.

## Cancellation Rate

Cancellation Rate reflects inventory accuracy and fulfillment readiness. Amazon publishes a target sellers should stay under (often cited near 2.5%). Common drivers:

*   Poor multi-channel inventory sync
*   Unreliable supply
*   Overstating available stock

Controls: buffer rules, conservative sellable quantities, and SKU-level checks during peak volume.

## Late Shipment Rate

Late Shipment Rate should stay under Amazon’s published target (often cited near 4%). At scale, sustained increases can trigger warnings before a serious enforcement notice.

Control habits:

*   Segment SKUs by fulfillment risk
*   Keep handling times aligned with real capacity
*   Move high-risk or complex FBM items only when operations can support them

## Valid Tracking Rate

Valid Tracking Rate is a credibility signal for shipped orders. Amazon expects a high share of valid carrier tracking (often cited above 95%).

*   Use carrier methods that produce valid tracking
*   Avoid unreliable manual tracking practices
*   Review carrier performance on a schedule

## Customer response time

Amazon expects timely buyer-message responses, commonly within 24 hours including weekends and holidays—confirm [current messaging guidance](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GX663LDD3TGU86GX) for your marketplace.

*   Cover inbox ownership on weekends
*   Prioritize urgent or complaint-heavy threads
*   Resolve in the first response when facts allow

Fast, accurate replies can reduce feedback and claim risk.

## Return dissatisfaction and listing quality

Return-related dissatisfaction often points back to listing accuracy, images, packaging, or expectations—not only “difficult buyers.” Control requires clear product content, accurate return instructions where applicable, and timely refund processing when policy requires it. Guide: [Amazon listing optimization](/amazon-listing-optimization/).

## Policy and compliance signals beyond the main gauges

Also watch:

*   Policy warning frequency
*   Listing suppression patterns
*   Repeat issue themes after appeals

A rise in warnings without an immediate penalty is still an early signal. Related: [how managers handle policy violations](/professional-amazon-account-managers-handle-policy-violations/).

## Build a performance control system

Do not rely only on Amazon notifications. Build internal habits that:

*   Track trends, not only red/green thresholds
*   Assign ownership for each metric
*   Trigger action before limits are reached
*   Document root causes when metrics move

Predictable process makes metrics manageable.

## Practical weekly checklist

1. Review ODR components and open claims
2. Check late shipment, cancellation, and valid tracking trends
3. Clear buyer messages within target response windows
4. Note new policy warnings and suppressions
5. Assign fixes with owners and due dates

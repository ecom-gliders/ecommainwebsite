## FBA inventory management: act now to reduce stockout risk

Soft claim: stockouts pause sales and can weaken organic rank and force costly recovery ads—**Assumed**. Soft claim: “now” means tighten cover this week; it does not mean stockouts never happen. Soft twin notes: practical checklist → [avoid stockouts](/fba-inventory-management-avoid-stockouts/); forecasting/warehouse timing → [inventory forecasting](/amazon-fba-warehouse-illustration-inventory-forecasting/); stability rebuild → [stockouts to stability](/fba-inventory-management-stockouts-to-stability/).

## Soft why stockouts happen

*   Underestimated demand or seasonality  
*   Lead time longer than assumed (production + freight + Amazon check-in)  
*   Late visibility—acting only when units are critically low  
*   Ads/promos that accelerate velocity without matching inbound  

## Soft do-this-week actions

1. **Cover check** — days of cover vs full lead time for every high-velocity SKU  
2. **Safety stock** — add a buffer sized to volatility (often ~2–4 weeks for many accounts—**Assumed**, not a rule)  
3. **Supplier ping** — confirm capacity and ship dates; escalate at-risk POs  
4. **Inbound plan** — create/ship before cover drops below lead time + buffer  
5. **Ads throttle** — pause aggressive spend on thin-cover ASINs  
6. **Alerts** — daily/weekly review of restock and inventory health reports  

## Soft recovery if already OOS or near-OOS

Restock with priority production or expedited freight when economics allow. Soft claim: after restock, velocity may need paid support or promos to recover—**Assumed**. Soft practice: write down the root cause (forecast, lead time, ads) and update the reorder rule so the same miss does not repeat.

## Soft principles (keep simple)

Data-driven forecast → buffer → monitor → reliable suppliers → automate alerts when catalog grows. Soft tradeoff: more buffer reduces stockout risk but raises storage/cash cost.

## Bottom line

Treat near-term stockout risk as an operations sprint: cover math, buffers, supplier action, and careful recovery. Soft claim: that reduces lost sales; it does not guarantee perfect availability.
